YOUTUBE DETAILS OPTIMIZATION

Use Metadata to Clarify the Episode and Support Discovery

These details are applied after the topic, thumbnail and title are decided. They help YouTube understand the subject, help viewers navigate the episode, and create a consistent searchable channel structure.

APPLY TO EVERY UPLOAD
HIGH PRIORITY

Title

Put the searchable problem or subject first. Keep guest name, episode number and “The Performance Gap” later. One clear promise is stronger than several competing keywords.

HIGH PRIORITY

Description opening

Use the first 1–2 sentences to explain exactly what the episode covers using natural topic language. Avoid beginning with a generic podcast description.

MEDIUM PRIORITY

Chapters

Name chapters by the question or concept discussed: “Acceleration vs Max Velocity” is more useful than “Topic 2.” Chapters improve navigation and create more searchable context.

MEDIUM PRIORITY

Captions & transcript

Correct guest names, coaching terms and technical phrases. Accurate captions improve accessibility and ensure the spoken topic is represented correctly.

SUPPORTING

Tags

Use relevant topic variations, names and terminology only. Tags are a supporting metadata field; do not spend more time on them than the thumbnail, title or first minute of the edit.

SUPPORTING

Hashtags

Keep them narrow and directly related to the episode. A practical starting point is 2–3 relevant hashtags such as #SportsPerformance #StrengthAndConditioning #SportsCoaching.

CHANNEL STRUCTURE

Playlists

Group episodes by viewer intent: Speed & Athletic Development, Coaching, Performance Psychology, Rehab & Recovery, Nutrition. This makes the channel easier to browse after one video ends.

CHANNEL STRUCTURE

Channel About / positioning

State who the channel serves and the subjects it consistently covers. Keep the wording aligned with actual uploads so the overall channel topic remains coherent.

TOPIC SELECTION FOR RECOMMENDATIONS

Choose One Relatable, Distinct Problem Per Video

Broad topics describe a category. Strong episode topics tell a viewer exactly what problem, question or outcome the video is about. This matters when YouTube recommends the video on Home, Suggested, or the Shorts feed because a new viewer must understand the value immediately without already knowing the podcast.

TOPIC FIRST, BRAND SECOND

Too general

These topics are valid fields, but they are too broad to create a strong reason to click when shown to a new viewer.

Relatable + distinct

These angles connect the subject to a clear problem, decision or outcome that a coach or athlete can immediately recognize.

BETTERHow Athletes Perform Under Pressure: Confidence, Focus & Decision-Making
BETTERWhy Some Athletes Are Strong but Still Not Fast
BETTERCleared to Play Doesn't Mean Ready to Perform
BETTERAre Athletes Eating Enough to Actually Recover and Perform?
1 · One viewerKnow who the video is for: coach, athlete, parent, practitioner, or a defined mix.
2 · One problemAvoid trying to cover speed, psychology, nutrition and recovery in one title or thumbnail.
3 · One promiseThe title and opening should tell viewers what they will understand, fix or improve.
4 · One visual ideaThe thumbnail should reinforce the same topic instead of introducing a second message.
Why this helps recommendation traffic: when YouTube surfaces a video to someone who does not know the channel, the viewer has only a few signals—topic, thumbnail, title and opening—to decide whether it is relevant. A specific topic makes it easier for the right viewer to recognize “this is for me,” while the episode itself can still explore the subject in depth after the click.

03 · VIDEO EDITING STRATEGY

Editing Rules That Can Be Applied to Every Episode

The edit should improve comprehension, authority and retention without turning a professional coaching podcast into an over-edited short-form video.

BEFORE EDITING

Build an edit map

Watch or skim the full recording once and mark the usable moments before detailed cuts.

  • Best 8–15 sec cold-open quote
  • Main teaching sections
  • Strong disagreement / myth
  • Guest story or case example
  • 5–7 short-form clip moments
0:00–0:15

Cold open first

Start with the strongest statement from the episode, not a long introduction.

  • Show the speaker immediately
  • Add clean captions
  • Use only relevant B-roll
  • Do not reveal the entire answer
0:15–0:20

Short brand sting

Keep SHSC / Performance Gap identification brief so the viewer reaches the conversation quickly.

  • 3–5 seconds maximum
  • Consistent logo animation
  • Original / licensed audio only
  • No repeated long intro later
MAIN PODCAST

Cut for clarity, not chaos

Use speaker changes, reactions and meaningful visual resets rather than random zooms every few seconds.

  • Remove false starts and repeated phrases
  • Tighten long dead air
  • Keep natural pauses that add meaning
  • Cut to listener reaction when useful
B-ROLL

Show what is being discussed

B-roll should explain or prove a point instead of acting as decorative filler.

  • Coaching sessions
  • Speed / strength drills
  • Testing or monitoring footage
  • Whiteboard / programming examples
  • Relevant athlete movement only
TEXT & GRAPHICS

Highlight useful information

Use graphics when they make a coaching concept easier to understand.

  • Key terms and numbers
  • Simple comparison graphics
  • Definitions for technical language
  • Guest credentials lower-third
  • Avoid covering faces or movement
AUDIO

Dialogue remains the priority

Professional audio consistency matters more than aggressive music or sound effects.

  • Remove obvious hum / noise
  • Balance both speakers
  • Prevent clipping
  • Keep music clearly below speech
  • Use the same loudness approach each episode
SHORT-FORM

Cut native clips, not trailers

Each vertical clip should make sense on its own to someone who has never seen the podcast.

  • 20–45 sec starting target
  • Hook in first 1–2 sec
  • One idea per clip
  • 9:16 reframing
  • Readable burned-in captions
QUALITY CHECK

Review before export

A final quality pass prevents small production mistakes from lowering perceived professionalism.

  • Correct guest / technical spellings
  • Check captions
  • Check cuts and sync
  • Check brand assets
  • Watch first minute again before export

Recommended long-form episode opening

0:00–0:15Strong guest / host quote
0:15–0:20Short SHSC brand sting
0:20–0:45Topic promise + why it matters
0:45+Conversation with purposeful cuts
ThroughoutMark clips, chapters and follow-up ideas
04 · EXECUTION PLAYBOOK

Upload Checklist + Decision Rules

Every upload follows the same operating checklist so the strategy can be executed by the client, editor or content manager.

YouTube upload checklist

✓Primary audience problem is clear in the first 30 seconds.
✓Primary keyword appears naturally near the start of the title.
✓Thumbnail communicates one idea at mobile size.
✓First description paragraph explains the actual episode topic.
✓Chapters use meaningful searchable topic names.
✓Captions checked; guest name and technical terms corrected.
✓Relevant playlist added: speed, rehab, coaching, psychology, nutrition, etc.
✓3–5 short-form moments marked for repurposing.
✓Thumbnail/title test prepared for priority episodes.

Analytics → action matrix

High impressions + low CTRRework title/thumbnail. Topic has distribution but packaging is weak.
Good CTR + weak 30-sec retentionOpening is not fulfilling the thumbnail/title promise fast enough.
Good retention + low reachTest a stronger topic angle, packaging or search phrase.
High saves/sharesCreate follow-ups and a repeatable series around the same problem.
High comments/questionsTurn repeated audience questions into the next episode or Short.
Shorts perform, long-form does notUse the winning short-form hook as the next long-form angle.
05 · SOCIAL MEDIA

Multi-Format Content → Multi-Platform Growth System

Use podcasts, gym exercise footage, lifting demonstrations, coaching clips, recovery tips, nutrition content, carousels and behind-the-scenes material as separate content sources. Each platform should receive a native version instead of an identical repost.

YT

YouTube Shorts

Use Shorts for fast discovery: podcast insights, exercise demonstrations, lifting mistakes, coaching cues, recovery tips and nutrition answers that can stand alone in under a minute.

  • 20–45 sec primary clips
  • Hook or visual payoff in first 1–2 sec
  • One problem, exercise or lesson per Short
  • Use podcast clips and original gym footage
Best content sourcesPodcast highlights, squat/deadlift demonstrations, gym technique corrections, quick recovery advice, simple nutrition Q&A and coach reactions.
PackagingUse a clear on-screen question or outcome such as “Why Your Squat Stalls” or “Do You Need Protein Right After Training?”
EditingOpen on movement or the strongest sentence, crop vertically, add readable captions and remove long intros.
Publishing goalReach new viewers first, then connect strong Shorts to related long-form videos, playlists or follow-up Shorts.
IG

Instagram

Build a visual education library with Reels, exercise demos, carousels, Stories and short coaching posts that people can save, share and revisit before or after training.

  • Exercise and lifting Reels
  • Carousel summaries and checklists
  • Stories: polls, Q&A, teaser and BTS
  • Strong cover text for grid readability
Best content sourcesTechnique demonstrations, before/after coaching examples, mobility drills, balanced-diet tips, podcast quotes, client-safe gym B-roll and training checklists.
Reels strategyLead with the movement or mistake on frame one. Keep the explanation practical and use native vertical framing.
Carousel strategyTurn one topic into 5–8 slides: problem → common mistake → correction → example → takeaway.
EngagementUse Stories to ask what followers struggle with, then turn repeated answers into the next Reel or carousel.
TT

TikTok

Use direct, searchable and conversational videos around gym questions, exercise form, lifting technique, recovery, food choices and coaching opinions. Content should feel native rather than repackaged.

  • 15–45 sec practical clips
  • Question, mistake or result immediately
  • Demonstration + explanation format
  • Reply-to-comment follow-up videos
Best content sourcesGym-floor demonstrations, reaction-style coaching breakdowns, exercise myths, recovery mistakes, meal/fueling explanations and concise podcast moments.
Search strategySay the search phrase naturally in the video and repeat it in on-screen text and caption, e.g. “how long to rest between sets.”
Native styleAvoid polished corporate intros. Start with the movement, question or strongest opinion.
Follow-upsUse high-performing comments as prompts for the next video so the account builds connected topic clusters.
FB

Facebook

Use native Reels and useful educational posts for a broader community audience: gym exercises, coaching lessons, recovery guidance, nutrition basics, podcast highlights and relatable training discussions.

  • Native Reels and short videos
  • Exercise / training education posts
  • Discussion-focused captions
  • Shareable coaching and recovery lessons
Best content sourcesExercise demos, longer educational clips, podcast takeaways, simple nutrition graphics, training myths and community-friendly fitness advice.
Caption strategyAdd enough context that the post makes sense without sound. Use one clear question to invite useful discussion.
Community reachFavor relatable topics that gym-goers, coaches and active adults can share with training partners or family.
RepurposingDo not simply repost an Instagram Reel with platform watermarks; export a clean master and upload natively.

Do not copy the YouTube title word-for-word everywhere. Keep the same topic, but package it in the language people naturally use when searching or scrolling for gym, exercise, coaching and fitness content.

06 · CONTENT MANAGEMENT ACROSS PLATFORMS

One Content System for Podcasts, Gym Videos & Social Posts

Manage podcast episodes, gym exercise demonstrations, lifting clips, coaching tips, recovery content, nutrition posts, carousels and behind-the-scenes assets in one trackable workflow. Every content item should have an owner, status, due date, final file and published URL.

Recommended multi-format content pipeline

1 · ResearchChoose the topic, search intent, audience problem, format and platform. Decide whether the idea works best as a podcast, exercise demo, lifting clip, recovery tip, nutrition post, carousel or short-form video.
2 · PlanDefine the hook, title/caption direction, thumbnail or cover concept, filming requirements, B-roll, exercise angles and expected deliverables.
3 · CaptureRecord the podcast, gym exercise, lifting set, coaching explanation, recovery demonstration, food/nutrition visual or behind-the-scenes footage needed for the content.
4 · OrganizeSave raw video, audio, photos, thumbnails, graphics, logos and notes into one clearly named content folder before editing.
5 · EditCreate the main version first, then cut native Shorts, Reels, TikToks, Facebook clips, carousels or Stories from the same source where appropriate.
6 · ReviewCheck exercise accuracy, guest names, technical wording, captions, nutrition wording, brand consistency, music rights and final export quality.
7 · SchedulePrepare the platform-native title, cover, caption, hashtags, description, tags and publishing time for each destination.
8 · Publish & EngageRecord public URLs, reply to relevant comments, save useful audience questions and identify follow-up content opportunities.
9 · Analyze & ReuseTrack views, retention, saves, shares, comments, clicks and follower growth. Rework strong topics into new formats instead of repeating the exact same post.
